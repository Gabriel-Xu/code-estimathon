Execuflight Flight 1526 was an Execuflight airline charter flight from Dayton Airport, Ohio to Akron Fulton Airport on November 10, 2015, with a Hawker 800. On final approach to Akron, the pilots lost control of the aircraft and crashed in a residential neighborhood and exploded. The nine occupants were killed in the crash, the aircraft and the house in which it had crashed, had burned completely at the accident site.

The crew consisted of 40-year-old Colombian Captain Oscar Chávez and 50-year-old Italian First Officer Renato Marchese. There were no flight attendants on the flight. The captain had been with the airline since June 4, 2015. His cumulative flight experience was 6,170 hours, of which he had flown 3,414 hours as pilot in command. He had 1,020 hours of flight experience with the accident plane, of which 670 hours as pilot in command. The first officer was hired by Execuflight on June 1, 2015. He had 4,382 hours of flight experience, 3,200 of which he had served as pilot in command. His flight experience with accident-type aircraft totaled 482 hours, which he had completed in its entirety as a first officer.

Flight 1526 took off from Dayton Wright Brothers Airport (MCY) to Akron-Canton Airport (CAK). Flight 1526 was cruising altitude at 17,000 feet (5,180 metres).

When they are descending to 13,000 feet (3,960 metres), the Captain checks the weather to the control tower and they reported winds at 290 degrees at 07 celcius, visibility at 2km, and temperature at 09 celcius, but unpredictable that they've overcast the weather in the Airport.

As the crew descending at 9,000 feet (2,740 metres), the controller reported the visibility changes at 1.

5 mist, weather condition 600 feet broken, and temperature 11 celcius. The Akron weather is getting worse if there's a possible chance to land in Akron-Canton Airport.

6 minutes from landing, the crew got a new distraction. The flight instructor was also teaching the pilot how to land in bad weather as the same runway as Flight 1526. The controller holds Flight 1526 onto the approach and requested heading 360, reducing speed 170 knots, and maintaining 3,000 feet which they need to hold their course and delaying their descent. After 2 minutes further delay on runway 25 localizer, Flight 1526 is cleared to land. The crew was notified that the piper had landed safely on the runway and the pilots are now ready to land. While approaching on the runway, the crew checks the final preparations onto the runway 25 localizer. As 2 miles from the runway, the pilots still searching when the visibility gets worsen. They need to have their safe minimum descent altitude until the pilots could see the runway. When the plane breaks through a thick clouds, they can see the ground clearly. As the plane still approaching and still looking at the runway, they've descended very steeply as they still looking on the runway. When they are below minimum descent altitude, the First Officer tried to push the throttle up, then the stick shaker and aerodynamic stall warning sounded when they are in danger above minimum approach onto the runway and which resulted the plane crashes in a 2-story residential building area.

Recordings and interviews with the colleagues of the pilots showed that the pilots were overly tired at the time of the accident, having only slept about six hours a night on average during the previous nights.

They lowered the aircraft below the safe altitude of 473 feet, although they could see the ground but still could not see the runway. As they searched for the runway, they failed to see that the airspeed had dropped to 98 knots, causing an aerodynamic stall.

The accident is featured in the 2nd episode of Season 21 of "Air Crash Investigation," also known as "Mayday." The episode is titled "Playing Catch Up".
