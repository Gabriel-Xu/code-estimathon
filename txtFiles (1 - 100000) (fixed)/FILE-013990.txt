Catain founded Enchanted Family Buying Company (EFBC) in Excelsior, Minnesota in 2002, which was later revealed to be a shell corporation. He funneled about US$12 billion through his company at Anchor Bank and collected about US$3 million in commission.

On October 3, 2008, Catain was charged with money laundering. On October 8, he pleaded guilty to money laundering on behalf of the former businessman and convicted criminal Tom Petters between 2002 and September 2008. The Petters Ponzi scheme has been included under historical examples on the list of Ponzi schemes. He then awaited sentencing. He had properties in Costa Rica. In January 2009, Catain was taken back into custody after violating the terms of his release by hiding assets from the court. He was accused of taking US$14,000 in proceeds from Excelsior Bay Car Wash, a car wash he owned. His home was searched and some items were removed, which were later returned. His assets were frozen, by a court-order. He once told Petters: "it rained last night and 18 head of cabbage grew", later confirming that he meant US$18,000 in cash had been delivered. Catain first met Petters in the mid-1990s, when Catain was running a retail liquidation business. Petters first approached Catain in 2000. Catain was later sued by GE Capital and put out of business. Catain testified against Petters in court on November 12, 2010. He was due to be sentenced after March 15, 2010. His sentencing date had not been scheduled by July 2010. He had no prior criminal record. He was ordered to be released from the Sherburne County jail by a federal judge on March 21, 2011. Catain's wife, Joan Kenney Catain, has been named in official court documents.

On September 13, 2010, Catain was sentenced to seven and a half years in federal prison on one count of conspiracy to commit money laundering. He had been released on a US$25,000 bond in October 2008 and jailed for two months in early 2009. He received credit for the two months he had previously served. He requested to serve his full time at the federal prison camp in Duluth, Minnesota. His release date was set for March 31, 2017.

Catain was released on April 21, 2017, according to the Federal Bureau of Prisons. He had been imprisoned at Duluth Federal Prison Camp. He spent three years on supervised release.

Catain founded lovepeacecows.

com upon his release from prison, and worked at Tinos Pizza, a family run pizzeria in Birmingham.

Catain married Joan Kenney Wolff (born 1956), a businesswoman, in Clark County, Nevada on January 3, 1981. They have two sons together; Michael Alan "Mike" Catain Jr. (born December 3, 1981), a chiropractor, and Nicholas Edward "Nick" Catain (born January 2, 1989), a psychologist.

Catain is the uncle of Todd Durboraw, a former actor and crew member.

Catain's father, Jack M. Catain Jr., married Kathleen Richards, a casting director, in 1980. Richards was Catain's stepmother, briefly. Catain Jr. and Richards divorced in 1982. Catain's mother, Marlene Katz, died from cancer in Las Vegas, Nevada on September 29, 2014. She was 80. Marlene married Byron Ellis "Al" Katz (1928–2019), a businessman, in 1986. Katz was Catain's stepfather.

Catain resides in Minnetonka, Minnesota.

In March 2011, it was reported that Catain was in poor health and would need hip and back surgery in the near future.

Cities: SkylinesCities: Skylines is a city-building video game. It was published by Paradox Interactive and was developed by Colossal Order. The game was first released on 10 March 2015 for Linux, OS X, and Windows. It was also later released on 21 April 2017 for the Xbox One, 15 August 2017 for the PlayStation 4, 13 September 2018 for the Nintendo Switch, 17 May 2022 for the Google Stadia, and 15 February 2023 for the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S.

When the idea of a full city simulation game first came up, Colossal Order had rejected the idea because they didn't believe that they could properly compete with the "SimCity" series. They decided to change their minds after they saw an opportunity with the failure of the 2013 "SimCity".

In the game, players build a city. They can control many things in the city such as taxes and zoning. With the inclusion of the "Natural Disasters" DLC, sometimes things such as earthquakes, tsunamis and other disasters can hit and destroy parts of the town. Players can use mods from the Steam Workshop to expand and increase the level's area to build.

There was a sequel game called "" on 24 October 2023.

The game was very well liked by both game critics and players. Critics said that they liked the low cost of the game and that it felt like a return to what made the city building genre great. Some even said that it had overtaken "SimCity" as the dominant game in the genre.

Since June 2022, "Cities: Skylines" has sold over 12 million copies worldwide. It is also the best selling game in Finnish game history.
