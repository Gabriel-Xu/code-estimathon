Jack M. Catain Jr.

Jack Michael Catain Jr. (May 29, 1930 – February 21, 1987) was an American businessman and convicted criminal. He became well-known through his connections to the American Mafia.

Jack Michael Catain Jr. was born in Cook County, Illinois on May 29, 1930, as the younger of two sons to Jack Michael Catain Sr. (1904–1978), a businessman, and Angelina "Angie" Catain (née Capoluongo; 1904–1958), a housewife. His elder brother was Salvatore Rocco "Solly" Catain (1928–2021), an electrician.

Catain Jr. graduated from Von Steuben High School, a public high school in Chicago, in 1946.

Catain Jr. founded an aluminum company upon moving from Chicago, Illinois to Los Angeles, California in the 1950s. He eventually headed the company as president when it was acquired by Rusco, a Westwood-based conglomerate with annual revenues of US$100 million. Catain Jr. and Rusco were the subject of police investigations around 1978. He denied the allegations against him. He stepped down from the company in 1980.

Catain Jr. was involved in businesses ranging from cosmetics and construction to exotic-car sales. He worked primarily as a San Fernando Valley businessman.

Catain Jr. was well connected to the mob, with links to the highest level of American Mafia "families" in Chicago, Philadelphia, Detroit, and New York. He was the focus of much police effort since, at least, 1976. He had been accused of money laundering, extortion, trading in stolen securities, counterfeiting, and ticket scalping. He worked as a loan shark. He was labeled a mobster by "The Wall Street Journal". He had been named a Genovese Mafia enforcer.

Catain Jr. was indicted on federal counterfeiting charges in 1982, but after numerous postponements, William P. Gray, a United States District Judge, dismissed the case against him in April 1986.

Catain Jr. was convicted at his first ever trial on federal racketeering charges of conspiring to sell part of a US$3.

3 million cache of counterfeit US$100 bills on November 7, 1986. He denied the allegations against him and accused the United States Secret Service investigators and prosecutors from the United States Organized Crime Strike Force of tampering with evidence. His sentencing was scheduled for December 8. He was released on US$50,000 bail. Catain Jr. was scheduled to be sentenced on January 28, 1987, but, was admitted to the hospital with a heart problem on January 27, and his sentencing was postponed for three weeks. On February 18, 1987, it was reported that his sentencing had been postponed for six months and scheduled for August 17. Catain Jr. died three days later while awaiting sentencing. He had faced up to 15 years imprisonment and US$225,000 in fines.

Catain Jr. was connected to Barry Minkow, a businessman, pastor and convicted felon. After graduating from high school in 1985, Minkow devoted all of his time to ZZZZ Best. Short of cash, Minkow got a loan from Catain. Catain later sued Minkow for not paying him his share of the company's profits, but Minkow claimed Catain was a usurer. The lawsuit was still working its way through the courts at the time of Catain's death.

Catain Jr. resided in Tarzana, Los Angeles. He had contributed to numerous charities. He was a registered Democrat.

Catain Jr. was married three times, and had two children from his first marriage.

Catain Jr. married Marlene Noble (1934–2014), an apparel sales associate, in Cook County, Illinois on May 29, 1953, his 23rd birthday. They had two children together; a daughter, Michele Catain (born November 6, 1953), a crew member and real estate agent, and a son, Michael Catain (born March 12, 1956), a businessman and convicted criminal. Their divorce was finalized on December 26, 1979. He had two grandsons, including Todd Durboraw. A third grandson was born two years after his death. Marlene's sister, Florence Joyce "Flory" Noble, was married to Catain Jr.'

s brother from September 25, 1953 until her death on December 22, 2018.

Catain Jr. married Kathleen Richards, a casting director, in Los Angeles, California on July 4, 1980. He gained three stepdaughters upon this marriage; Kathy Hilton, Kim Richards and Kyle Richards, all of whom were actresses. Their divorce was finalized on March 16, 1982.

Catain Jr. married Phyllis Joan Sherwood (née Dorne; born May 12, 1935), a performer, singer and actress, in Los Angeles, California on May 1, 1983. He gained two grown stepsons upon this marriage; Michael Sherwood and Billy Sherwood, both of whom were musicians, the sons of Bobby Sherwood, an actor. She worked as the president of Beauty Sales Inc. and Le Mid during their marriage. The couple separated in 1985 and were married at the time of his death.

Catain Jr. had a long history of medical problems. He survived two heart attacks and had artificial hips. He had also been diagnosed with terminal heart disease. He suffered from a variety of heart ailments. He underwent open heart surgery.

Catain Jr. died while in the cardiac intensive care unit at Encino Hospital Medical Center in Los Angeles, California on February 21, 1987. He was 56. His death was confirmed by a nursing supervisor, but no further details were announced. His attorney, James A. Twitty, confirmed that he had been hospitalized for heart surgery.

Catain Jr. was interred at the San Fernando Mission Cemetery in Mission Hills, Los Angeles, California.

Count Henrik of MonpezatCount Henrik of Monpezat (born Prince Henrik of Denmark; 4 May 2009) is a member of the Danish royal family. He is the third and youngest son of Prince Joachim and the only son of his second wife, Princess Marie. He is a grandson of Queen Margrethe II and Prince Henrik and a nephew of King Frederik X.

Henrik is eighth in the line of succession to the Danish throne.

On Ascension Day, Thursday 18 May 2023, Count Henrik was confirmed in Frederikskirken-The Danish Church in Paris. The confirmation took place at 11:00 administered by the royal Chaplain-in-Ordinary, bishop Henrik Wigh-Poulsen, with the assistance of Frederikskirken's minister, Malene Bendtsen.
