He was concurrently named titular archbishop of Foratiana and President of the Pontifical Mission Societies upon his appointment as Adjunct Secretary.

He was concurrently serving on the Norwalk Common Council and was an active policeman as well.

He was concurrently the Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary to Cape Verde, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Mauritania and Senegal and High Commissioner to Gambia.

He was concurrently the mayor of Santa Ana and the city attorney of Placentia from 1973 to 1975.

He was condemned at the First Council of Ephesus (431) and appealed to the pope in 433.

He was condemned by a council of his enemies and expelled from his see, though he was able to return there to live quietly with a small congregation in the last years of his life.

He was condemned by Archbishop William Warham and by Bishop John Fisher and burned at the stake at Maidstone on 23 February 1530.

He was condemned by the governor of Pannonia, and then beheaded on 24 March 304.

He was condemned by the Lex Mamilia, which was passed to punish all those who had been guilty of treasonable practices with Jugurtha.

He was condemned for heresy and burnt at Smithfield that year.

He was condemned in absentia to life imprisonment for the assassination of Aldo Moro's escort in 1978.

He was condemned in Algeria to a life sentence, charged of criminal association, corruption, abuse of trust, and forgery.

He was condemned in the third letter of Pope Hadrian I for adoptionism sometime between 785 and 791 (probably 786).
