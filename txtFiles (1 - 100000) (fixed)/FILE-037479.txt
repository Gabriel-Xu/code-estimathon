In 1987 she appeared in a children's television program called Jossy's Giants about a fictional youth football team, playing Opal, girlfriend of goalkeeper Harvey.

In 1987 she appeared in the film Obsessed and was nominated for the 1989 Genie Award for Best Performance by an Actress in a Leading Role.

In 1987, she appeared on the MCA Records release of the Andrew Lloyd Webber original cast recording album Music & Songs from Starlight Express, appearing on four of the album's tracks.

In 1987 she appeared with the Los Angeles Master Chorale at the Beethoven Festival, alongside Michael Zearott and Thomas Wilcox.

In 1987, she became the first African American Radio City Music Hall Rockette.

In 1987 she became the first female president of the American Society of Criminology.

In 1987, she became the first South Korean model for the Japanese sports drink brand Pocari Sweat.

In 1987 she became the first Westerner to be accepted by the Hanguk Kiwon as a pro; she remains one of only five western females ever to attain professional status (with Joanne Missingham, Svetlana Shikshina, Diana Koszegi and Mariya Zakharchenko).

In 1987 she began composing again, and also became a production coordinator and assistant director with the New South Wales Department of Education.

In 1987, she began working for Johns Hopkins Hospital, where she was asked if she'd like to work for their new clinic made to help women with HIV.

In 1987 she created the AIDS Task Force, an international foundation where she was executive director until 1993.

In 1987 she established the Atalante dance theater, together with two other choreographers, Eva Ingemarsson and Gunilla Witt.

In 1987, she founded the American Indian Language Development Institute at the University of Arizona, resulting in the linguistic training of many indigenous language educators and promoting the development and revitalization of native languages in Arizona and throughout the country.

In 1987 she founded the first Festival Flamenco Internacional de Alburquerque, a festival which has continued into the present.

In 1987, she founded the international academic journal Bioethics with Peter Singer.

In 1987, she founded the Linbury Biennial Prize for Stage Design to identify and encourage talented newcomers to the field of theater design.

In 1987 she founded the Reto Medical Center at Sultan Hamud.

In 1987 she graduated from the University of Amsterdam and in 1988 she became chair of the Trade Union for Education.

In 1987 she joined CKND to "assume responsibility for TV news and current affairs programming".

In 1987, she left academia to work in industry as directory of biostatistics at Quintiles, but returned as an assistant professor in Community and Family Medicine in 1991, and added a joint appointment in Anesthesiology in 1996.
