Many of the texts are rosters of weekly shifts or courses of temple service for the twenty-four priestly families, known as Mishmarot.

Many of the texts have not been translated into English before.

Many of the texts relate to Jerusalem, Judaism and Israel.

Many of the texts were translated to English for the first time.

Many of the thefts were blamed on Native Americans, and Musgrove was known on occasion to disguise himself as an Indian to thwart law enforcement officers.

Many of the them are shaped by the effects of impacts into ice-rich ground.

Many of the themes are about racism and other ills in the black community.

Many of the themes of this novel and in large part the title are re-borrowed from the 19th Century gothic novel Melmoth the Wanderer by Irish author Charles Maturin.
