Nelson was drafted by the Green Bay Packers in the second round of the 2008 NFL Draft.

Nelson was drafted by the Milwaukee Bucks in the fifth round of the 1971 NBA Draft and spent the 1971-72 season with the team.

Nelson was highly active during all that time: preaching, confirming, and consecrating persons, especially in the northern mission of the diocese in the Adirondack Mountains.

Nelson was hired by Ed Fredkin's Information International Inc. to work on PDP-7 programs.

Nelson was inducted into the UW Husky Hall of Fame in 1998.

Nelson was inspired to create the annual concert after his participation in the 1972 Dripping Springs Reunion, that was hosted at Hurlbut Ranch in Dripping Springs, Texas.

Nelson was inspired to write the song during one of his trips from his home in Pasadena, Texas, to his work, singing at the Esquire Ballroom in Houston.

Nelson was later awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom award in recognition of his work.

Nelson was married to Carl Rave, an ironworker and union activist who also participated in birth control activism.

Nelson was named in the 2017 World Cup squad.

Nelson was one of the most conservative Democrats in the Senate, frequently voting against his party.

Nelson was on the 1984 US Olympic team for judo.

Nelson was ordered to send Calder home, and allowed him to return in his own flagship, even though battle was imminent.

Nelson was president of Central Washington University (CWU) from 1992 to 1999.

Nelson was president of the Institute of Food Technologists (IFT) for 2001-2.
