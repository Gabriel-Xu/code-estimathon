This enzyme is required for terpenoid biosynthesis in some organisms, since it is a key enzyme on the MEP pathway for the production of the isoprenoid precursors IPP and DMAPP.

This enzyme is required for the destruction of mitotic cyclins and for cell cycle progression.

This enzyme is responsible for de novo DNA methylation.

This enzyme is secreted by Pseudomonas sp.

This enzyme is secreted enzyme from Clostridium histolyticum.

This enzyme is thought to function in the maintenance and processing of CAAX-type prenylated proteins.

This enzyme is thought to play a role in the onset and progression of neurodegenerative human disorders, including Alzheimer disease and multiple sclerosis, and it has also been implicated in glaucoma pathogenesis.

This enzyme is thus the target of the widely available cholesterol-lowering drugs known collectively as the statins.

This enzyme is upregulated in mammalian cells in response to stress induced by heat shock and tunicamycin treatment.

This enzyme localizes to the inner mitochondrial membrane, where it performs the NADP+-dependent dehydrogenase/cyclohydrolase activity as part of the mitochondrial pathway to convert folate to formate.

This enzyme may also play a role in hair follicle formation.

This enzyme may also present in animals and plants, including mouse.

This enzyme may be involved in the processing of bacterial peptides and endogenous proteins for MHC class II presentation in the lysosomal/endosomal systems.

This enzyme participates in 2 metabolic pathways: citric acid cycle, reductive citric acid cycle (CO2 fixation), and is also important in renal cell carcinoma.
