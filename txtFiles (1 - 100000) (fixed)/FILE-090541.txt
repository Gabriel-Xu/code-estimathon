As police secure the area around the Paris offices of Charlie Hebdo, family members and journalists look to find answers. WSJ’s Noémie Bisserbe reports from the scene. Photo: Getty ImagesThe last tweet on Charlie Hebdo’s Twitter feed, posted Wednesday morning, shows a cartoon drawing of Islamic State leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi giving his best wishes for 2015. “Best wishes by the way,” the tweet reads. “And health above all,” Mr. al-Baghdadi is portrayed as saying in the cartoon.

On the cover of Wednesday’s edition, the weekly featured French writer Michel Houellebecq, whose latest book “Soumission,” imagines a France run by a Muslim president. “In 2022 I will do Ramadan,” the writer says in the drawing.

Also in the issue, a cartoon by Mr. Charbonnier carries a headline saying “Still no attacks in France,” and showing a jihadist soldier carrying an AK-47 warning: “Wait! We’ve got until the end of January to convey our wishes.”
