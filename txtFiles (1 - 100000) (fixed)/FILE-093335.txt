The rating agencies, Obama’s punching bag for missing the signs of the housing collapse, are proving to be hyper-vigilant when it comes to the politics of the debt ceiling; S&P says the chances of a downgrade are 50-50, even if the parties raise the ceiling and cut some spending.

Administration officials, echoing the carping of Euro zone pols, are mounting a PR pushback but Obama faces the prospects of a AA future, which would be devastating fiscally and well-nigh disastrous politically.

Can anyone envision a more devastating line than “Barack Obama, the man who lost America’s AAA credit rating — and brought you 9 percent unemployment”?

Even Republicans concede that Obama has looked pretty good so far, and done a masterly job positioning himself as the most responsible adult in the room and a fiscal hawk, despite years of Keynesian commitment to stimulus spending.

Yet the notion that the embattled speaker is the only one who’s lost control isn’t true. Instead of projecting the image of a clutch fourth-quarter performer who always wins, Obama now looks like a jilted prom date waiting, in ruffled tux, for a date that never arrives — or a phone call that’s never returned.“

I think that one of the questions that the Republican Party is going to have to ask itself is, Can they say yes to anything?” an exasperated Obama asked reporters on Friday night after the “grand bargain” heaved its last breath. “And so then the question becomes, where’s the leadership?”

Others might ask him the same question. By kicking the ball back to Capitol Hill and essentially demanding that Boehner come back to him with a bill, a president who prides himself on control and responsibility has essentially handed both to Republicans he’s already branded as “irresponsible.”
