Vocal Cord Haemorrhage results when one of the blood vessels on the surface of the vocal cord ruptures and the soft tissues of the vocal cord fill with blood. It is considered a vocal emergency and is treated with absolute voice rest until the haemorrhage resolves.

If left untreated the condition may result in scarring of the vocal folds, which is a condition that is much harder to treat and has permanent effects on voice quality. In cases where a haemorrhage produces a polyp - or blood blister - on the vocal cords that doesn’t go away, the polyp can be removed with microlaryngoscopic surgery. Doctors use a laryngoscope to see the vocal cords, and microscopic instruments are inserted through the mouth. No incisions are made in the skin, and general anesthesia is required for the one-hour procedure.
