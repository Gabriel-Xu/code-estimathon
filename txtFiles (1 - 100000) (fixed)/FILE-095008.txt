The world's most important astronomical authority has slapped down a bid to hawk the names of Mars' craters, saying the red planet is not up for sale. The move came after Colorado-based crowdsourcing site Uwingu said the public could name craters on its map of Mars for a fee. 'Such initiatives go against the spirit of free and equal access to space, as well as against internationally recognised standards,' said Paris-based International Astronomical Union (IAU). Founded in 1919, the IAU holds the official mission of naming all planets, satellites, comets and asteroids in the Solar System. The topographical features of Mars, named after the Roman god of war, for instance, carry Latin names like 'mons' for mountain and 'planitia' for plain.
