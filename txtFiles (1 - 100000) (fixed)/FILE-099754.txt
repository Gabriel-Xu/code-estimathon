Equality Florida statement on mass shooting at Orlando gay club:We are reeling from the tragic news that a gunman opened fire on the 2am capacity crowd at Pulse leaving 49 people dead and over 50 injured according to Orlando Mayor Buddy Dyer.

We are heartbroken and angry that senseless violence has once again destroyed lives in our state and in our country.

Gay clubs hold a significant place in LGBTQ history. They were often the only safe gathering place and this horrific act strikes directly at our sense of safety. June commemorates our community standing up to anti-LGBTQ violence at the Stonewall Inn, the nightclub that has become the first LGBTQ site recognized as a national monument.

We have received a steady stream of emails and messages from those seeking to help or to make sense of the senseless. We make no assumptions on motive. We will await the details in tears of sadness and anger. We stand in solidarity and keep our thoughts on all whose lives have been lost or altered forever in this tragedy.

Austin, home to a vibrant gay and lesbian community, mourned in private and public Sunday as news quickly spread of the early morning massacre at a gay nightclub in Orlando, Fla.

At an evening interfaith vigil at the Capitol that included Muslim leaders, a Christian pastor and other religious figures, Austin Mayor Steve Adler told more than 300 gathered that there is strength in diversity.“

It is too easy to find fear in our differences,” Adler said. “Let’s choose unity over division, let’s choose confidence over fear, let’s choose lightness over darkness, and let’s choose love over hate.”

Sheikh Mohamed-Umer of the Nueces Mosque said he wanted to make it clear that the Austin Muslim community will not tolerate “any sort of hate” toward people of different religions, backgrounds, races or orientations.“

Every single member of the LBGTQ community is my brother and my sister,” he said. “Let the rainbow shine after a rainstorm and let compassion and love prevail.”

Isabella King, a transgender activist who moved to Austin from Orlando, urged the crowd to avoid letting the actions of “one crazy person” divide the community.“

I woke up this morning not knowing how many of my friends are dead in that nightclub,” she said. “We have to be better than the hate. We have to be better than the anger.”
