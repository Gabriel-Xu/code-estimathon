The school was funded by Pierre S. du Pont as part of a reform and rebuilding of African-American schools in Delaware, between 1919 and 1928.The school was funded by Shoe4Africa to honor the world track and field 800m champion, and Olympic Silver medallist, Janeth Jepkosgei who had attended the former school in the 1990s.The school was funded directly by central government and had a planned intake of 16 pupils per year.The school was further honored in 1997 with the establishment of the George Bush Presidential Library on the western edge of the campus.The school was geared toward ensuring these high-risk potential dropouts graduate with the honed abilities to succeed in college.The school was Gilbert's first high school, opening in 1918.The school was given a "B" grade for accountability by the Indiana Department of Education in 2016.The school was given a reprieve, and was eventually closed at the end of the 2010-11 school year.The school was given its current name in 2010.The lower academy facility is a historic school building designed by the architect Richard Kiehnel of Kiehnel and Elliott.The school was given to the city in Murchison's memory by his widow, former teacher Harriet Murchison Beckwith (1855-1927).The school was governed by a board of trustees, appointed by the President of the United States, upon the recommendation of the Attorney General.The school was graded "Outstanding" in its 2012 Ofsted report and is listed in the top 50 schools in the United Kingdom.The school was granted academy status in August 2011.The School was granted a royal charter in 1952 and merged with UCL in January 2012.The school was granted full authorization as an IB World School by the International Baccalaureate Organization in April 2012.The school was granted specialist status for cognition and learning and awards include Investors in People, Careers Education and Guidance Quality Mark, ArtsMark, Eco School and the Healthy School Award.The school was handed over to Nazareth Charles in 2006.The school was highly praised in its Ofsted report for its anti-bullying strategy.The school was highly regarded for its academic and military programs, and many notable American political and military leaders are graduates, including 1964 presidential candidate Sen. Barry Goldwater, 1960's folk singer Phil Ochs, and John Dean, a White House Counsel who was a central figure in the Watergate scandal of the early 1970s.The school was honored in 2012 and 2013 by the Washington Post High School Challenge Index as one of "America's Best High Schools".The school was housed at the Valley Park School while a new campus was under construction.The school was housed in a former fortification and battery called South Battery which was constructed prior to the War of 1812.
