A billionaire New York City art dealer is refusing to return a famous $25 million painting that was stolen from a renowned Jewish art dealer by the Nazis during World War II, a sensational lawsuit charges. In court papers, Philippe Maestracci alleges that the Helly Nahmad Gallery -- located in Manhattan’s posh Carlyle Hotel on Madison Avenue -- is wrongly in possession of the 1918 painting, 'Seated Man With a Cane,’ by Italian artist Amedeo Modigliani. The canvas was owned by Maestracci’s grandfather Oscar Stettiner, a prominent Jewish gallery owner in Paris. But Stettiner was forced to flee with . his wife and two children months before the German troops stormed into . Paris and the north and west of France became occupied Nazi territory. 'Adolf Hitler, a failed artist . himself, then began the most notorious art looting raid in the history . of the world', Raymond Dowd, the lawyer for Maestracci, told MailOnline. When the war ended, all sales of art stolen by the Nazis were declared null and void’ under international law.
