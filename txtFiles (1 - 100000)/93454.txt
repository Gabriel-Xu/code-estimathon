There were hugs and tears in a south Texas courtroom on Tuesday when a judge set bail of $50,000 (£32,000) for Hannah Overton, who has maintained her innocence since she was found culpable in the death of a four-year-old boy. Her 2007 trial attracted nationwide attention and prompted claims that she was wrongfully convicted.Members of Overton’s church were organising payment of the bail amount, according to the Corpus Christi Caller-Times, which first reported the hearing’s outcome. The prosecution had reportedly sought a $250,000 bond.The judge, Mario Ramirez Jr, did not set a restriction limiting Overton’s contact with her children after hearing several hours of testimony from witnesses who praised her character.In prison she was allowed to see her family once a month but not make physical contact. Ramirez ordered those involved in the case not to speak to the media.Overton’s conviction for capital murder was overturned in September by the Texas court of criminal appeals, which found that the defence’s failure to present testimony from a sodium poisoning expert seriously undermined the original trial’s outcome.Her family’s joy at the news was tempered when in response, rather than seek a lesser charge or dismiss the case, the Nueces County district attorney, Mark Skurka, opted to prosecute again on the same charge. A trial is expected to take place next year.“No jury, no trial judge and no appellate court has ever found that defendant Hannah Overton is not responsible for the death of Andrew Burd,” Skurka said in October.Andrew had been living in Corpus Christi with Hannah and Larry Overton pending formal completion of his adoption by the pair, a devout Christian couple with no prior criminal history. The boy, who had behavioural problems, suddenly developed breathing difficulties, fell into a coma and died in hospital in October 2006.Prosecutors alleged the Overtons were culpable for his death, arguing at a televised trial that Hannah was an ill-tempered parent who disciplined the child for bad behaviour by fatally force-feeding him with spicy seasoning.
