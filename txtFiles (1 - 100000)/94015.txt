The seismic activity related to the latest Kilauea volcano eruption decreased today, even though the lava outbreak in Leilani Estates remains active, according to an update tonight from the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory.HVO scientists said that while earthquake activity at Kilauea’s summit remains elevated, there were about 31 magnitude-2 earthquakes at depths less than 5 3 miles beneath the summit over the 24 hours ending at about 9 p.m., which was a decrease compared with the previous 24-hour period with 152 magnitude-2 and magnitude-3 quakes.The fissures in Leilani Estates, meanwhile, continue to spew lava through the lower Puna subdivision on the Big Island.Fissure No. 8 erupted with lava fountains until about 4 p.m. today, and the lava flow advanced north through the afternoon, even after the fountains shut down, HVO scientists said. The flow crossed Ho’okopu Road about 0.6 miles from the fissure, they said. New ground cracks near fissures No. 8 and 9 were emitting steam and gases, but scientists observed no lava spattering from them.6:45 p.m.Hawaii County police and prosecutors said they “have established a policy of zero tolerance toward looting or vandalism,” which will be treated as a felony.The warning comes as the lower Puna communities of Leilani Estate and Lanipuna Gardens, with about 1,800 residents, are under an evacuation order.Hawaiian Volcano Observatory and county officials said tonight that a total of 10 fissures have emerged in Leilani Estates, destroying at least 26 homes and a total of 30 structures.However, Leilani Estates residents will be allowed to continue to temporarily return to their homes from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. each day, until further notice, so that they can retrieve medicines, pets and other essential items.
