The dollar, being a global currency, is needed by most countries, including China, to act as a stabilizing force.More than 60 percent of U.S. dollars are circulated outside the United States and this money will never return. In fact, the trade deficit with China and the dollar are not directly related.From the trade point of view, China actually depends on the U.S. more than the U.S. relies on China. While our exports are mainly in the financial services industry — banking, insurance, investments, etc. — and large commodities, like planes produced by companies like Boeing, China is an exporter of consumer goods purchased largely by Americans.In recent years, the Chinese economy has slowed. It’s not as stable as that of the U.S. China needs the U.S. dollar as its reserve currency in order to stabilize its economy, and it wants to continue to be able to export commodities to the U.S.When Apple produces iPhones and other products in China, most of its parts are produced elsewhere and imported to China for assembly. Without other countries (including the U.S.) supplying those parts and without U.S. consumers buying the phones, the Chinese would be in a poor financial position.Further, U.S. consumers depend on the inexpensive goods they buy from China, from apparel to household items and electronics.
