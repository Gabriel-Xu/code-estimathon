Many of Italy's oldest specimens, some 500 years old, have been infected by the bacteria, named Xylella fastidiosa. The species causes plants to dry out, leaving shrivelled stumps that are incapable of bearing fruit. The epidemic has already cost producers €250m (£200m) - a figure expected to rise. Officials have called on experts from the University of California, Berkeley, to help them contain the devastating outbreak.
