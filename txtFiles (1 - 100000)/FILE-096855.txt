A picturesque village immortalised in the paintings of John Constable is at the centre of a middle-class crime mystery following the introduction of controversial parking charges. Dedham - which lies on the Essex and Suffolk border - had all the coin slots in its parking meters filled will glue in an apparent angry reaction to the local council's bid to bring in charges. The village - which featured in a number of Constable's most famous paintings and has been declared an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty - has long been free to park in. The village's attractive main street is lined with Georgian-fronted houses, old inns and a large art centre . The River Stour, which flows through the village, is hugely popular with tourists during the Easter and summer breaks . But many feel tourist numbers will plummet due to new parking charges introduced by the local council . But . council bosses recently voted to end the free scheme in favour of an . unpopular policy of charging people on the busiest roads. The . changes, introduced last month, led to an outpouring of opposition from . local traders, who fear charges of up to £4 for parking will lead to a . significant fall in visitor numbers. It . now appears that someone has taken action into their own hands after, . following the Easter weekend, all the payment slots on the new parking . meters were mysteriously filled with foaming glue, making them . impossible to use. Dedham's . traders have denied they are responsible, suggesting instead that irate . tourists who have enjoyed years of free parking may have committed the . crime.
