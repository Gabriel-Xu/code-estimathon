In 1995, he approached sculptor and patron of sculpture J. Seward Johnson Jr. with the idea of creating a state-of-the-art stone-carving studio, so that American sculptors would not have to travel abroad to realize their work.In 1995 he argued that the American news media approach religion with certain Western religious preconceptions that do not always do justice to the varieties of religious belief and behavior.In 1995 "Heartbreak Hotel" was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and in 2004 Rolling Stone magazine named it one of the "500 Greatest Songs of All Time".In 1995 he became a Fellow of The Royal Society of Edinburgh.In 1995, he became a founding director of the Center for Ecoliteracy in Berkeley, California.In 1995 he became a lecturer at Oxford University.In 1995, he became a mechanic and rear tire changer for Sadler Racing.In 1995 he became a member of the National Academy of Sciences.In 1995 he became a member of the national team.In 1995 he became an artist in residence at the Chapungu Sculpture Park, where he still works.In 1995 he became a works BMW driver in the German Supertouring Championship, coming second in the series, and also winning the Spa 24 Hours.In 1995, he became director of Design for GM Asia Pacific, overlooking collaborative projects with Daewoo, Suzuki, Fuji Heavy Industries and Isuzu.In 1995, he became foreign correspondent in Washington, D.C. then moved to South Africa before returning to Canada in 1999.In 1995, he became General Secretary of the International Confederation of Free Trade Unions (ICFTU), a post he held until 2002.In 1995 he became its Vicar.In 1995 he became MLA on congress party's ticket.In 1995, he became one of the founders of California Proposition 215, the initiative that created the first medical cannabis framework in the United States.In 1995 he became Pro-Vice-Chancellor and then Deputy Vice-Chancellor of UMIST until 2002.
