The buildings were constructed between 1895 and about 1945, and represent stylistic influences ranging from late Victorian period examples displaying elaborate brick-corbeled cornices and pediments to the more simplified and minimalist Depression-era examples with typical low relief detailing and vertical piers.The buildings were constructed during the 1630s and 1640s, and were the political center of the Orthodox people of Volyn.The buildings were constructed following a fire that destroyed much of the Clinton commercial district.The buildings were constructed in 1911, the year the Verdi Lumber Company was incorporated.The buildings were constructed in 1941 by Public Works Administration and Civilian Conservation Corps labor.The buildings were constructed in brick and are largely two to three stories tall.The buildings were converted to apartments in 1988-1991.It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1995.
