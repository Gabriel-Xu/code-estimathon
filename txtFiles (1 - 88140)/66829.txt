The center was jointly sponsored by the Association of Village Council Presidents (AVCP) and the University of Alaska Fairbanks (UAF) and at the present the center operated by the UAF's Kuskokwim Campus, AVCP and City of Bethel.The center was known by the code name "GODLIKELESION".The center was known for centuries as simply Tocco, and the name "da Casauria" was added only after 1861.The center was launched in 2000 by Ivy Singh-Lim and her husband, former NTUC FairPrice chief executive, Lim Ho Seng, as a project to run after they had both retired from their previous careers.The Center was launched in December 2007 at Mumbai to facilitate research, technology and educational partnerships between Imperial College and Indian organizations and businesses.The center was launched May, 2006, and consists of researchers from the School of Interactive Computing in the College of Computing, College of Engineering, and Georgia Tech Research Institute.The Center was led by Richard "Riggs" Corriere and Joseph Hart, who would ultimately be stripped of their licenses to practice psychology as a result of the fallout from the group's collapse.The center was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2005.The center was located on the Indiana University campus in Bloomington, Indiana, United States.The Center was located on the PNG Navy Base Lombrum (previously a Royal Australian Navy base called HMAS Tarangau) on Los Negros Island in Manus Province, Papua New Guinea.The center was made of compressed paper held between two plate-iron disks.The Center was made up of independent members, intellectuals and professionals from different academic disciplines.The center was managed by Northern Territory Correctional Services, an agency of the Department of Justice of the Government of the Northern Territory.The Center was meant to embody the University's commitment to fostering multicultural understanding through an appreciation of Asian cultures.The center was modeled after a "rural" Hawaiian community, with its grocery store modeled after a "country store".The center was modeled on the Mall of America and West Edmonton Mall centers and was developed by Old Mutual Properties.The Center was named after Suzanne Mubarak, wife of former Egyptian president Hosni Mubarak, who was interested in improving the physical and social well-being of women in Egypt.The center was named after the Pulitzer prize winning poet and physician William Carlos Williams.The center was named for Houston Harte, founder of the Harte-Hanks media company and a contributor to the university.The center was named for internationally recognized entrepreneur, engineer, civic leader, philanthropist and Houstonian George Rufus Brown (1898-1983).The center was named for SuAnne, who had wished for a "Happy Town" for children in her community.The Center was named for UConn president Albert N. Jorgensen, who oversaw construction.The center was named in honor of Cecil B. Day, a philanthropist and the founder of Days Inn.
