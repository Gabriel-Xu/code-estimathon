The shell of Corona perversa is normally left-handed or sinistral.The shell of Cyrtonybyoceras is curved slightly upwardly and is slightly compressed.The shell of Darvasiceras is discoidal, moderately involute in adult stages; the venter narrow, smooth, and flat; the umbilicus shallow and small.The shell of Domatoceras is evolute, coiled with whorls touching but not overlapping, some growing to be moderately large.The shell of Dosinia species is disk-like in shape, usually white, and therefore is reminiscent of the shells of Lucinid bivalves.The shell of Eophyllites is evolute, discoidal; whorls compressed, deeply embracing; flanks gently convex, converging on a narrowly rounded venter.The shell of Eotripteroceras is a depressed orthocone with the underside (the venter) flatter than the upper (the dorsum).The shell of Eubostrychoceras is a loosely to tightly wound spiral forming a corkscrew with an open, empty umbilicus in the middle.The shell of Falcitornoceras is strongly involute, lacking an umbilicus.
