The Shell offers concerts on Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday nights during its spring and fall seasons.The shell of Fuciniceras is generally small, evlute, and strongly ribbed.The shell of Griesbachites is involute, subglobose to subdiscoidal, ribbed; with sloping sides converging on an arched venter.The shell of Hindioceras is described as large, gyroconic, with about 2 volutions, whorls being barely in contact.The shell of Kachpurites is subinvolute with a rounded section and bears low, close spaced, moderately sinuous ribs.The shell of Kammerkaroceras is involute, all whorls except for the outermost hidden from view, discoidal with sigmoidal ribs that branch on the sides and cross over the rounded venter.The shell of Kilianella is evolute with a slightly grooved venter and covered by strong, gently flexious, single or bifucating ribs.The shell of Landeroceras is a subcylindrical brevicone with the siphuncle on one side, determined as ventral.The shell of Michiganites is discoidal and smooth, as for the family.The shell of Microbaltoceras is straight (orthconic) and tiny, with the siphuncle in narrow contact with the inner ventral surface.The shell of Minganoceras is a slender depressed exogastric cyrtocone, curved so that the underside, the venter, is longitudinally convex and the opposite side, the dorsum, is longitudinally concave.The shell of Ochetoceras is rather involute and strongly ribbed.
