The website uses its own proprietary publishing platform with video content.The website uses video interviews, vintage photographs, original research reports and other materials to chronicle the eventful history of the organization from its influence in the reform movement of the 1930s to the red scare of the 1950s and beyond.The website viewers are estimated to be more than 9 million per month.The website was acquired by IAC's Match.com division in 2011.The website was also recognized as an official Nominee for Webby Awards in the Religion & Spirituality category for the 2011, 2012, 2014 and 2015 Webby Awards, and as an official Honoree for Best Editorial Writing in 2016.In October 2013, Religion Dispatches moved to the University of Southern California's Annenberg School for Communication and Journalism's website.The website was also recognized by Family Tree Magazine as being one of the 101 Best Genealogy Websites of 2012.The website was also used for dating and discovering new events, bands and hobbies.
