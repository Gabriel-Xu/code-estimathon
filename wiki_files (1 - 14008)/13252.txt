It describes the relationship between the temperature and volume of a gas, if the pressure and amount of gas do not change. It is also known as the Law of Charles and Gay-Lussac or the Law of Volumes.According to Charles's Law, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to its temperature when measured at a constant pressure. In other words, as the temperature of a gas increases, its volume increases, and as the temperature decreases, its volume decreases. This relationship can be written mathematically as:V₁ / T₁ = V₂ / T₂where V₁ and T₁ are the initial volume and temperature, and V₂ and T₂ are the final volume and temperature.Charles's Law assumes that the pressure and amount of gas do not change.
